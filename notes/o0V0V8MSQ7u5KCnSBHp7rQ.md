## Decide Which Problems the New Installation Should Solve

Before choosing a model, make a note of the issues you experience with your current boiler. This gives the installer a practical starting point for discussing replacement options.

For instance, you may have noticed that the existing appliance breaks down regularly or that repairs have become increasingly expensive. Alternatively, the boiler may still work but no longer provide enough hot water for the way your household operates.

Not every problem requires a new boiler. A qualified heating engineer should assess whether the fault can be repaired economically and whether the existing appliance remains suitable for your property.

If replacement is recommended, ask what the new system is expected to improve. This helps you compare the available options based on your actual requirements rather than relying on general claims about efficiency or performance.

## Match the Boiler to Your Water Usage

The number of rooms in your home is only one part of the selection process. Your household's hot-water habits, incoming water supply and available equipment space also matter.

### Combi boilers

A combination boiler heats water on demand and provides central heating without a separate hot-water storage cylinder. It can suit properties where space is limited and the appliance can meet the household's demand.

However, its suitability depends on the available mains water flow and how often different hot-water outlets are used simultaneously. Discuss these factors before deciding on a model.

### System boilers

A system boiler works alongside a separate hot-water cylinder. This arrangement may be suitable when a household needs stored hot water for several uses, provided the cylinder has appropriate capacity and sufficient space is available.

### Conventional boilers

Some properties use a traditional setup with a boiler, hot-water cylinder and separate cold-water storage. Retaining this arrangement may be sensible if it continues to meet the property's requirements.

Changing from one type of system to another can involve additional plumbing and other alterations. Ask your installer to explain the practical implications before approving a design.

## Check the Existing System Before Removing Anything

The old boiler is not the only component that matters when planning a replacement. Pipework, radiators, heating controls and other parts of the system can affect the installation requirements.

An installer should assess whether the existing components are suitable for continued use and identify any work needed to support the new appliance. Where remedial work is recommended, ask why it is necessary and whether it is included in the quotation.

The boiler's proposed position also needs careful consideration. A replacement in the same location may require fewer alterations than moving it to another room or cupboard. Any new position and flue arrangement must meet applicable safety requirements and the manufacturer's instructions.

If you are renovating a kitchen or extending your property, agree on the boiler location before other work progresses too far. This can help avoid conflicts with cupboards, plumbing connections and electrical installations.

Residents in Kingston, Surbiton, New Malden, Tolworth and surrounding neighbourhoods should provide their full postcode and explain any access restrictions when requesting a survey. The installation requirements should be assessed at the property rather than assumed from its location.

## Compare Installation Quotations on Equal Terms

A quotation is easier to assess when it clearly explains the complete scope of work. Two installers may offer the same boiler model but include different materials, controls or preparation work.

Before accepting a price, check whether it covers:

- The specified boiler, labour and removal of the existing appliance.
- The agreed pipework changes, materials and heating controls.
- Installation testing and commissioning.
- Relevant installation records and compliance arrangements.
- Warranty terms and any registration requirements.

Also ask whether system cleaning, repairs to existing components or relocation will incur additional charges. If further work becomes necessary, request a clear explanation and agree on the cost before authorising it.

A straightforward replacement may take around a day, although relocation and more extensive alterations can require longer. Your installer should explain the expected schedule based on the work planned.

## What to Check When the Installation Is Complete

Ask for a demonstration of the heating controls and make sure you understand how to adjust the temperature and schedule. If the new boiler operates differently from your previous appliance, this is the right time to ask questions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. How long does a boiler installation take?

A straightforward replacement may take around a day. Relocation or major system changes can take longer.

### 2. Can I change from a conventional boiler to a combi?

Possibly. The installer must assess your water supply, hot-water demand, pipework and available space.

### 3. Do I need a Gas Safe registered installer?

Yes. Gas boiler installation must be completed by an appropriately qualified Gas Safe registered engineer.

### 4. Does a new boiler installation include radiators?

Not automatically. Radiator replacements or upgrades should be agreed and listed in the quotation.

Keep the commissioning record, warranty information and applicable compliance documentation.
