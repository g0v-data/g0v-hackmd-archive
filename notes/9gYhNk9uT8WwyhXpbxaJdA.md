# 10 Things to Check Before Buying a Sea View Apartment in Dubai

A sea view is one of the most attractive features a Dubai apartment can have. Waking up to open water, watching sunsets over the Gulf and having a balcony that feels like a resort are strong reasons to pay more. But a view is also one of the easiest things to oversell. Photos can be taken from the best angle, a "partial sea view" can mean a thin strip of blue between two towers, and a view that exists today may disappear when the plot next door is built on.

This guide gives you ten practical checks to complete before you pay a deposit. If you are comparing sea view apartments for sale in Dubai, use them as a working list and tick off each point in order.

## 1. Confirm What the View Actually Shows

Not all sea views are equal. Listings use many labels, such as full sea view, partial sea view, marina view, Palm view or waterfront view, and these terms are not standardised.

- Stand on the balcony and in the main living areas, not just in the photographs
- Check whether the view is direct, angled or only visible from one corner of the balcony
- Look at what else is in the frame, such as other towers, roads, car parks or construction
- Confirm the floor and the exact unit, since the view can change a lot between a mid floor and a high floor

Ask the agent to specify the view in writing, and compare it with what you see in person. For overseas buyers, request a live video from the balcony rather than relying on pre-recorded footage.

## 2. Find Out What Could Block the View in the Future

This is the most important check, and the one buyers most often skip. A clear view today is not guaranteed forever.

- Ask which plots in front of or beside the building are undeveloped and what is planned for them
- Review the community masterplan and ask the developer or agency to explain future phases
- Look for cranes, hoardings and early construction in the surrounding area
- Check whether the beachfront or waterfront in front of your building is protected from further building

A view over a public beach, a marina channel or open water with no available plots is more secure than a view across land that is still waiting to be developed. Where a view premium is being charged, make sure you understand how durable it is.

## 3. Visit at Different Times of Day

A flat that looks perfect at 11 a.m. can feel very different at 4 p.m. or at night.

- Check how much direct sun enters the living room and bedrooms in the afternoon, since west-facing sea views can bring strong heat and glare
- Test the air conditioning and note how hard it has to work
- Listen for noise from the beach, roads, nearby venues, boats and building works
- Look at the view after dark, when lights, signage and traffic may change the atmosphere
- Visit on a weekend if the area attracts crowds

Balcony size and shading also matter. Check whether the balcony is usable in summer, and whether there is shade, glazing or screening.

## 4. Verify the Agent, the Agency and the Advertisement

Premium listings attract fake adverts and unlicensed brokers, so verify the people before you discuss money.

- Ask for the agent's broker card number and the agency's registration details
- Confirm both through the Dubai Land Department's official channels
- Check that the advertisement carries a valid permit number that matches the property, the agent and the agency
- Be cautious of sea view listings priced far below comparable apartments in the same building

A low price on a desirable view is a common lure in fake listings, so a bargain deserves extra scrutiny rather than extra excitement.

## 5. Verify the Owner and Match the Unit to the Official Record

For a ready apartment, make sure the person selling actually owns it and that the unit on paper matches the unit you are shown.

- Ask to see the title deed and compare the owner's name with their identification
- If someone acts for the owner, request a valid power of attorney and confirm that it covers the sale
- Compare the building name, unit number, floor, size, parking and number of bedrooms with the official record
- Ask whether the property has a mortgage, and request the bank's settlement figure and liability letter if so

Any mismatch between the listing, the title deed and the actual unit should be explained before you continue.

## 6. Benchmark the Price and the View Premium

Sea views usually command a premium, but the size of that premium should be justified by evidence, not by the agent's enthusiasm.

- Compare recent registered transactions for similar units in the same building, on different floors and with different views
- Look at price per square foot, adjusted for floor, size, finish and the quality of the view
- Ask how much of the asking price reflects the view and how much reflects the apartment itself
- Remember that asking prices show what sellers hope to receive, while registered sales show what buyers have actually paid

If a unit is priced significantly above similar apartments, find out whether the premium is justified. If it is far below, find out why.

## 7. Review Service Charges and the Condition of the Building

Waterfront living comes with particular maintenance demands, and they can affect running costs.

- Ask for the current service charge per square foot, the history of increases and a statement showing that dues are paid
- Inspect the facade, balcony railings, glazing and windows for wear, since salt air and humidity can age buildings faster
- Check for signs of leaks, damp, rust, peeling paint or ceiling stains, especially near balcony doors and windows
- Ask about planned major works, such as facade repairs, that could lead to special charges
- Review the building's management quality, lifts, security and common areas

Beachfront and branded buildings can carry higher service charges for the amenities and services they provide, so calculate your net cost of ownership, not just the purchase price.

## 8. For Off-Plan Units, Check the Developer, Escrow and the View Clause

Buying a sea view off-plan means buying a promise, so the protections matter even more.

- Confirm that the project is registered, that the developer is licensed and that an escrow account exists for the project
- Review the developer's delivery record and previous projects
- Check what the sales agreement says about the unit's floor, orientation and view, and whether the developer can change the design or surroundings
- Read the clauses on handover dates, delays, specification changes and resale
- Pay only to the official escrow account named in the agreement, never to a personal account

Renderings often show an idealised view. Ask for the actual floor plan, unit position and site orientation, and be sceptical of any promise about a view that is not written into the contract.

## 9. Calculate the Total Cost and Confirm Your Financing

The price on the listing is not the final cost. Request a written estimate of every charge before you make an offer.

- Dubai Land Department transfer fee, commonly 4 percent of the price
- Registration and trustee fees
- Agency commission, commonly around 2 percent plus VAT on a resale purchase
- Mortgage registration, arrangement and valuation costs if you finance
- Annual service charges, cooling or utility charges and insurance
- Furnishing, maintenance and any renovation

If you need a mortgage, get pre-approval first. A bank valuation can come in below the agreed price, particularly where the price includes a large view premium, so you may need to cover the gap with a bigger down payment.

## 10. Check Rental and Resale Demand, and the Building's Rules

Even if you plan to live in the apartment, think about how easily you could let it or sell it later.

- Look at how often similar sea view units sell and how long they stay on the market
- Compare rents for comparable apartments in the same building, and calculate net yield after service charges and vacancy
- Ask whether short-term or holiday-let rules apply to the building and what permits are required
- Check any building regulations on balcony use, furnishing, pets and alterations
- Consider how your view compares with other units, since a premium unit usually has to compete with the best in the building when you sell

A view that is clearly better than average tends to support resale and rental demand, while a marginal "partial view" may not hold its premium.

## Red Flags to Walk Away From

- Listings that promise a sea view but show only a heavily angled or cropped photo
- Prices far below similar units in the same building with no clear explanation
- Agents who refuse to share broker card, permit or ownership details
- Pressure to pay a deposit immediately, often with claims of a last unit
- Requests to pay outside official channels or into a personal account
- Off-plan promises about views that do not appear in the agreement
- Documents with mismatched names, missing stamps or unusual errors

## Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the most important thing to check when buying a sea view apartment in Dubai?

A: Verify what the view really shows and whether it could be blocked in future. Visit the exact unit, check the surrounding plots and masterplan, and get the view described in writing.

Q: Does a sea view add a lot to the price of an apartment in Dubai?

A: Sea views usually carry a premium, but the amount varies by building, floor and the quality of the view. Compare recent registered transactions for similar units to see whether the premium is justified.

Q: Can the sea view be blocked after I buy?

A: It can, if there are undeveloped plots nearby that are later built on. Ask about planned developments in front of and beside the building, and prefer views over protected areas such as open water or public beachfront where possible.

Q: Are there extra running costs for a sea view or waterfront apartment?

A: Service charges can be higher in beachfront and branded buildings, and coastal conditions can increase maintenance needs. Ask for the charge history, any planned major works and the building's maintenance record.

Q: How do I avoid scams when buying sea view apartments for sale in Dubai?

A: Work with a licensed agency, verify the broker card and advertisement permit, check ownership or project registration, and pay only through traceable, official channels. Be cautious of prices that look too good to be true.

Q: Should I buy a sea view apartment ready or off-plan?

A: Ready apartments let you see the actual view and condition before you pay. Off-plan units can offer payment plans and modern design, but you rely on plans and promises, so check the developer, escrow account and the agreement carefully.

Q: Do I need a lawyer to buy a sea view apartment in Dubai?

A: It is not mandatory, but legal review is wise for higher-value purchases, off-plan deals, transactions involving a power of attorney and purchases made from overseas.
