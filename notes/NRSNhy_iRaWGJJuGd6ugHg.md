<h2>Affiliate basics</h2>
<p>An online casino affiliate brings traffic and potential customers to an operator and earns a commission for customers who sign up or wager. That core idea is unchanged. What changed is the paperwork and the compliance focus. The Gambling Commission requires operators to hold a licence under the Gambling Act 2005, and affiliates are squarely part of that compliance chain when they steer customers to a site that serves Great Britain.</p>
<p>You will be judged by the operator's controls as much as by your own. The regulator treats email and SMS acquisition by affiliates as a customer-acquisition risk, so keep lists tidy and auditable. Operators must follow LCCP, the Data Protection Act 2018, and PECR when affiliates are used for direct marketing, and you should be able to show how you meet those standards if asked.</p>

<h2>Regulatory duties</h2>
<p>Operators must stop affiliates marketing to people who have self-excluded. The Gambling Commission expects gambling businesses to prevent affiliates from contacting or directing self-excluded individuals back to gambling sites, and to remove those people from marketing lists. That requirement extends to anyone working with data that might identify a self-excluded person.</p>
<p>Affiliates should not assume the operator will do everything. Keep records of where leads came from, what consent you relied on, and how you verified age and identity if you handle those steps. Treat the work as if you will be asked to produce those logs at short notice. The regulator keeps a close eye on how customer-acquisition risks are managed, and sloppy chains attract investigations.</p>

<h2>Player protection</h2>
<p>GAMSTOP is central to protecting players in Great Britain. GAMSTOP allows a customer to self-exclude from online operators with one request, and every operator that serves UK customers must offer clear self-exclusion options and their own exclusion arrangements. As an affiliate, make GAMSTOP clear where relevant and avoid promotions that could steer excluded customers back to gambling.</p>
<p>The industry also runs affordability checks. Operators must carry out appropriate checks to detect harm or unaffordable play. Affiliates who push high-value sign-ups without flagging risk are making life hard for operators and themselves. Remember: GamStop and affordability checks are standard. Mention them in your copy where they apply.</p>

<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<p>New affiliates often underprice compliance. They think a low-cost sign-up list is acceptable and expect the operator to clean it. This is a mistake. Operators will expect evidence of consent and that people on your lists are eligible to gamble. If you pass a problematic lead, the operator must investigate and may reject the commission or worse, flag you to the regulator.</p>
<blockquote>Practical rule: never send a list to an operator unless you can prove where each contact came from and show the consent used to reach them.</blockquote>
<p>Another frequent error is ignoring country filters. Some traffic sources do not differentiate between jurisdictions, and that can land a UK operator with players who are not meant to be served. Always test traffic by country and keep geolocation checks in place.</p>
