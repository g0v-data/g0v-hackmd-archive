# Solving Phone Reception Issues in Basements and Modern Extensions

The desire for additional living space has prompted a massive surge in ambitious home improvement projects across Ireland. Families are choosing to expand their current properties rather than endure the stress of moving house. We are seeing incredible ground-floor extensions featuring massive open-plan kitchens, deep basement conversions transformed into luxurious home cinemas, and solid garden rooms designed as high-end fitness studios. These architectural achievements add immense value and enjoyment to a property. However, once the builders pack up their tools and the fresh paint dries, homeowners frequently discover a highly frustrating hidden flaw. These newly created, beautiful spaces are almost always complete black holes for cellular communication.

The very nature of how we build downwards or extend outwards guarantees a severe drop in reception. If you have converted a basement, your new room is entirely encased in dense, damp earth and incredibly thick retaining walls made of poured concrete. Radio frequencies from local network masts simply cannot penetrate through several feet of soil and foundation blocks. Similarly, large modern extensions require heavy steel support beams to hold up the existing structure, along with highly insulated flooring slabs and thick masonry walls. When you sit on your new sofa in the extended living area, you are effectively sitting inside a dense bunker. The beautiful architectural choices that make the room structurally sound also completely block any chance of receiving a text message or an important incoming call.

This lack of connectivity quickly sours the enjoyment of your newly renovated space. You might have built a dedicated home gym at the back of the property, only to find you cannot stream your favourite workout playlists or answer an urgent call from the office between sets. If you use your new basement as a playroom for teenagers, they will inevitably complain that they cannot reach their friends, rendering the expensive room largely unused. Relying on your home broadband router to provide Wi-Fi calling is often insufficient in these scenarios, as standard routers struggle to push a strong signal through fresh concrete slabs or down into subterranean levels without complex mesh networks. You need a dedicated solution that directly addresses the lack of cellular radio waves.
