<h2>Understanding licensing and regulation in Great Britain</h2>

<p>The Gambling Commission licenses and regulates remote gambling—that's the official term for online or phone gambling that uses key equipment within Great Britain. Any website or app that trades or advertises to customers in Great Britain must hold a current Gambling Commission licence to be legal here. That's the law since the Gambling Act 2005, as amended.</p>

<p>I checked what this means in practice. A licence tells you the operator has met baseline standards on fairness, on how they hold player money, and on how they respond to complaints. The Gambling Commission also requires licensed operators to run affordability checks—they need to assess whether you can afford to gamble before they let you keep playing if losses mount. They must also offer their own self-exclusion tools on their site, separate from the national scheme.</p>

<p>What a licence doesn't guarantee is that you'll win, or that bonuses will be generous, or that the games suit your taste. I found that licence holders range from well-established international brands to newer entrants, and the quality of customer service, game selection and user experience varies widely. A licence is a floor, not a ceiling.</p>

<h2>Self-exclusion: GAMSTOP and operator tools</h2>

<p>Here's where the regulation genuinely helps players. The United Kingdom has two layers of self-exclusion. The first is GAMSTOP, the national online self-exclusion scheme. Since 31 March 2020, every Gambling Commission-licensed operator must be part of it. That means when you register with GAMSTOP, it blocks your access to gambling websites, apps and services provided by licensed operators across Great Britain, not just one site.</p>

<p>You can choose a self-exclusion period of 6 months, 1 year, 5 years, or 5 years with automatic renewal. GAMSTOP is free to use, and registration takes up to 24 hours to become effective. The scheme reported a 16% year-on-year increase in self-exclusion registrations in the first half of this year, with May setting a new monthly record, which suggests more players are using it than before.</p>

<p>The second layer is the operator's own self-exclusion tools. Licensed casinos must have their own arrangements in place. The Gambling Commission requires them to close your account if you self-exclude and return any money in it to you. But here's the catch—operator self-exclusion only applies to that one site. If you want to block yourself from all licensed operators, GAMSTOP is the tool that does it.
